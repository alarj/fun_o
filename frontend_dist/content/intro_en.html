Welcome to the world of orienteering!

fun-o.eu is a location- and question-based playful competition platform. You can create a fun course for your team, colleagues, class or group of friends, where participants move from point to point in a city or in nature and answer questions.

Why this format?

Orienteering is a great way to stay active at a comfortable pace, whether running or walking.
Taking part in an orienteering course does not require special skills or equipment, especially in an urban setting. All you need is a map and checkpoints placed in the terrain, and you can get started.
Traditional orienteering uses a printed paper map and checkpoints in the terrain that are also marked on the map.
This is exactly where the challenge appears, and what this application aims to solve:

  • The course must be planned, which means choosing suitable terrain in a city or in nature;
  • a map is needed, usually printed on paper. At least in Estonia, many orienteering maps have been created and can also be found online, but they are often not freely usable from a copyright perspective;
  • the course must be prepared in advance, checkpoints placed in the terrain, and everything collected again after the event.

Planning is a creative task and can be enjoyable to do even in your free time. What is much less enjoyable is spending roughly the same amount of time marking the course beforehand, and later another hour or more cleaning up markers and compiling results, just to support about an hour of physical activity for a couple of dozen people.
In an urban setting there is also the issue that attaching your own markings, even temporarily, to a building or structure owned by someone else without permission may not be the most law-abiding thing to do.
A separate challenge is how to verify checkpoint visits on the course. Very professional tools exist for this, such as SI chips and stations, but that still requires an arrangement with the owner of such a system, for example an orienteering club.

But what if you want to organize something for a couple of dozen friends or colleagues? Hiring a separate organizer feels excessive and turns an otherwise ad hoc event into a carefully planned project.
So the question is: can this be done more simply?
It turns out that yes, it can!

  • There are many permanent MOBO courses in Estonia that can be completed using a mobile phone both for viewing the map and marking checkpoint visits;
  • there is plenty of freely available map material in Estonia, neighboring countries and worldwide, and some of it is suitable for orienteering, especially in urban environments;
  • Last but not least, orienteering does not always need to be a serious sport. It can also focus on accessible movement and enjoyable time outdoors.

From all of this, an idea was born several years ago and has now taken on a new form through this application:

  • Checkpoints do not need to be physically marked in the terrain. It is enough if they exist on the map and the object near them is reasonably identifiable;
  • the map does not need to be printed, it can be viewed on a mobile phone;
  • for verifying checkpoint visits, a fun or educational question works very well, especially one that is easiest to answer correctly by actually going to the location. For example: "What color are the bicycle racks in front of the building?", "How old was the person on the memorial when they died?", "Is there a playground, a dog park or just a green area at this checkpoint?"

The first version of a similar orienteering game was created using Google Forms and Google Sheets. Maps were printed on a regular office printer, and later at a print shop because ordinary office paper quickly becomes unreadable outdoors in wet weather. At each checkpoint, participants selected the correct question in Forms to submit their answer. Responses were saved into Google Sheets, where a tangle of formulas produced the results page and everything else that was needed.
It was fun and it worked, but it still felt incomplete.

What can you do as an organizer?

  • Create a competition and its description, and define its start and end;
  • invite friends to organize the event with you, because working together is more fun and usually faster;
  • add checkpoints and connect them to the map if needed;
  • create questions and answer options, and assign points for correct and, if you want, even incorrect answers;
  • decide whether participants must visit checkpoints in a fixed order or can choose both the checkpoints they visit and the overall course length themselves;
  • decide which maps participants can use. The selection is already quite rich, even if not always at elite-orienteering level, and should continue to improve over time;
  • use orienteering maps generated from Estonian Land and Spatial Development Board open data. To create a suitable map for yourself, see the Est-Omap application;
  • decide whether participants must physically be near the checkpoint to answer questions, or whether guessing is allowed;
  • view results in real time both by participant and by checkpoint, and produce a summary and determine winners with just a few clicks.

What can participants do?

  • Register for a competition using the code received from the organizer. They do not need to reveal their real name or other personal data, a chosen alias is enough;
  • complete the prepared course using a mobile phone both for viewing the map and making checkpoint entries;
  • receive real-time feedback about their progress on the course;
  • enjoy themselves and, depending on the questions and answers, hopefully gain some new knowledge and a chance to compare results with others.

How do you get started as an organizer?

  1. Open the application and sign in with Google;
  2. Create a competition to match your idea and style;
  3. Share the competition code with participants.

Have fun!
